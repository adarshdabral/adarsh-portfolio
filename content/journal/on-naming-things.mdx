Spent twenty minutes today deciding between `getUser`, `fetchUser`, and `loadUser` for a function that does one `fetch()` call. Settled on `getUser`, because `fetch` already implies "goes over the network" and `load` implies "populates some cache," and this does neither of those extra things — it just returns a promise.

Is this a productive use of twenty minutes? Debatable. But a codebase where names mean exactly one consistent thing is a codebase where you can trust the names, and stop re-reading implementations to double check. That trust compounds.

## The rule I landed on

- `get*` — cheap, synchronous-feeling, might hit a cache
- `fetch*` — explicitly goes over the network
- `load*` — populates some larger structure or store as a side effect

Three words, one job each. Worth the twenty minutes.
